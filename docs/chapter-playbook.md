# Chapter Starter Playbook

This playbook is for people who want to establish a local Mandarin-English Social Club chapter using the same free model.

## Goal

Create a beginner-friendly in-person language exchange group that is:

- social
- structured
- repeatable
- low-pressure
- free to join and free to replicate

## What To Preserve

Local chapters can adapt venues and local details, but the core method should stay intact:

- people prepare before arriving
- each session has one clear topic
- speaking happens in the language being learned
- participants rotate regularly
- the structured part leads into relaxed social time

## Basic Operating Model

### 1. Choose an accessible evening format

Good first venues:

- cafes
- food courts
- libraries
- parks
- museum precincts
- walking routes with a clear meeting point

Optimise for ease, safety, and convenience. Do not over-design the setting.

### 2. Publish the topic before the session

Every session should have one narrow topic, for example:

- introducing yourself
- hobbies
- work or study
- travel
- food
- weekend plans
- Sydney or local city life

Share a small phrase list or prompt sheet before the meetup so people arrive ready to speak.

### 3. Ask participants to prepare a short script

Preparation is a feature, not a crutch.

- Mandarin learners prepare in Mandarin
- English learners prepare in English
- people can read from their phone if needed

The objective is spoken repetition and comfort, not performance.

### 4. Run the session in three phases

#### Warm-up

- welcome people
- explain the topic
- do short introductions

#### Guided speaking

- pair people up or make small groups
- have them use their prepared script
- rotate every 10 to 15 minutes
- keep reminding people to speak in the language they are learning

#### Social time

- dinner
- drinks
- takeaway
- walking and talking

This is where structure turns into real social connection.

## Facilitation Principles

- beginners should never feel embarrassed for reading notes
- the organiser should actively rotate people
- keep the room moving; do not let fixed cliques dominate
- keep explanations short and practical
- protect the supportive tone of the room

## Quality Bar

A strong chapter feels:

- welcoming
- clear
- practical
- culturally open
- socially alive

A weak chapter feels:

- vague
- cliquish
- overcomplicated
- passive
- too dependent on fluent speakers

## Minimal Chapter Checklist

- choose a venue
- publish a topic
- share prompts in advance
- ask people to prepare a script
- rotate every 10 to 15 minutes
- end with unstructured social time

## Notes On Cost

The model should remain free to use and free to replicate. Local organisers can choose simple venues that do not require booking costs or paid membership structures.
