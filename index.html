For beginners

You do not need to be fluent. You can read from your phone, use prompts, and build confidence by speaking out loud.

For regular practice

The format makes it easier to keep showing up, meet new people, and turn language learning into part of your social life.

Why people come back

  • Short prompts remove the fear of not knowing what to say
  • Partner rotation gives everyone multiple chances to practise
  • The structure supports beginners without making it feel like a class
  • Social time makes the conversations feel natural and memorable

Why join

Not a classroom. Not a random social night.

Most learners already know they need more speaking practice. The harder part is finding a place where speaking feels realistic, supportive, and worth returning to. That is what this meetup is designed to solve.

Who it is for

English speakers, Mandarin speakers, and anyone willing to try.

You do not need polished grammar or confidence on day one. If you are learning Mandarin, learning English, or interested in cultural exchange, the format gives you a practical way in.

Approach

A session rhythm that gets people speaking quickly.

01

Prepare before the session

People receive a topic and a small phrase set in advance so nobody arrives blank.

02

Start with a clear task

Introductions and warm-up prompts create an easy first win and lower the social barrier immediately.

03

Rotate speaking partners

Repeating the same idea to new people builds fluency, confidence, and adaptability.

04

Shift into relaxed conversation

Food, coffee, parks, libraries, museums, and city walks turn language practice into a real shared experience.

Philosophy

The point is confidence through repetition, not perfection on display.

Speaking comes first

The goal is not to impress anyone. The goal is to practise out loud, regularly, in front of real people.

Structure is kindness

When a newcomer has prompts, timing, and a clear sequence, participation becomes realistic instead of intimidating.

Culture belongs in the room

Language learning becomes stronger when tied to places, food, humour, routines, and everyday social life.

Open beats exclusive

The best version of this model is one that people can reuse freely, adapt responsibly, and spread without permission barriers.

The feel of the group

Language practice should feel alive, local, and social.

Dinner Coffee Parks Museums Libraries Walks
Speaking together

Some sessions happen over takeaway or dinner. Others can happen in libraries, parks, museums, or simply while walking around the city. The point is to connect speaking practice with real places and real interaction.

The structured section gets the room moving. The social section is where conversations loosen up, friendships form, and language starts to feel natural instead of forced.
